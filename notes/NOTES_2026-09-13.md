## The requested voice handoff is at a word boundary after preparation (2026-09-13 01:06, issue #95)

The owner accepted the surrounding recommendation but replaced sentence
restart: old audio keeps playing until the new audio is ready, then the
old voice finishes a word and the new voice takes over at the next word.
The earlier direct-select recommendation does not implement this behavior.
The issue body now records the corrected requirement. No code was changed.

The installed reader's `_getAudioData` (:40329) fetches, decodes and caches
audio and timestamps. `_playAudioBuffer` (:40019) accepts a buffer offset,
time-stretches for speed, and schedules word highlighting from timestamps.
This provides ingredients for a prepared handoff, but no exposed operation
atomically prepares a different voice and transfers playback at a text
boundary. `selectVoice` still destroys the existing controller immediately.

The two voices must be aligned by original text offsets, not equal audio
times or equal timestamp-array indices: pronunciation and timing differ.
The new audio must also be decoded and ready to play before committing.
The old controller's audio clock is the progress reference. Its
`ActiveWordChange` events (:40099) are wall-clock timers for word onset,
not audio-accurate word-completion events. Browser audio supports scheduled
starts and offsets ([Web Audio](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio/#dom-audiobuffersourcenode-start));
the implementation must account for time stretching and late callbacks,
not stop the old source merely because a highlight timer fired.

Some provider results have no word timestamps (`SynthesisResult.timestamps`
is optional; OpenAI, Cloudflare and Fish Speech declare no word timings).
The remote interface's whole-segment stand-in is not evidence of a word
boundary. Missing, overlapping or differently grouped timestamp ranges
can prevent an exact next-word match even when a provider advertises word
timings. The recommendation is to use a sentence boundary in those cases,
pending the owner's decision; never estimate a word boundary from elapsed
time or character count.

A future implementation also needs request generations so rapid key
presses select the latest target, cancellation on stop/tab close/manual
voice selection, handling for pause/seek/speed changes during preparation,
and revalidation when old playback has advanced beyond the prepared
sentence. A failed preparation should leave the old voice playing and
report the failure. Preparing audio that becomes obsolete can cost an
additional synthesis request. These are research findings, not a finalized
implementation plan or verified playback behavior.

## A prepared native controller carries the new voice to a safe boundary (2026-09-13 01:28, issue #95)

The owner accepted sentence-boundary fallback and authorized implementation.
`core/voice-switch.ts` mirrors the player's stable cost sort and matches
word boundaries by original text ranges. It rejects missing, overlapping,
out-of-range, phrase-level and partial-word timing rather than guessing.
`read-aloud/voice-switch.ts` prepares a paused native controller through
`_getAudioData`; the existing source keeps playing throughout preparation.
The new context must be running before the old source is scheduled to stop.

An aligned word handoff schedules `AudioBufferSourceNode.stop(when)` from
the old audio clock and timestamp end. Its ended event commits the already
prepared native controller through the ordinary manager selection. A
temporary `getController` shadow hands that controller to Zotero's own
listener wiring; its first buffer play uses the new voice's matching word
offset. A sentence handoff instead intercepts the old `_speakInternal` at
the prepared upcoming index, after the native sentence delay. If reading
overtakes a request, preparation advances farther ahead, bounded to the
current selection. Different segment granularities or controllers without
preparable audio currently require a paused voice selection and produce a
failure notice during playback.

Requests coalesce for 120 ms and time out at 60 seconds; the whole handoff
wait is bounded at 120 seconds. Pause, seek, speed, manual voice/language/
tier changes, controller destruction and shutdown restore all temporary
methods and destroy the unused controller. Repeated keys advance from the
pending target. Cancelling an armed stop reschedules it beyond natural
buffer completion; a catalog change racing that stop resumes the old buffer
at the completed word boundary. Discarded responses never start playback.

The memory layer's optional defer callback prepares other playing tabs
only during a shortcut-originated selection. Their existing native restore
still chooses the language and voice; unrelated manual picks retain their
existing behavior. Diagnostics expose the requested voice, prepared segment
indices and the final handoff kind, source/target IDs, text offset and audio
offset. The native ended event is dispatched on the main thread, so a busy
thread can add an audible gap even though the scheduled old source ends at
the intended audio time. Naturalness and perceptual gap still require
listening; automated tests do not establish that.

## Manual navigation waits for sentence disappearance (2026-09-13 13:46, issue #100)

immediate disengagement. A shared manual-follow gate is armed only by the

never imply manual intent. It stops the current animation and suppresses
follow requests until the gesture settles. Pointer/touch holds and pending
navigation Promises extend that interval; the 180 ms quiet period coalesces
wheel/key input, rather than distinguishing automatic from manual movement.
