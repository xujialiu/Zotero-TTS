## A reader whose window is gone: what dies, what reads fine, and the walks that stopped at it (2026-09-25 13:00, issue #143)

Diagnosed on a loop that runs the whole `startup()` of `src/index.ts` in
vitest against a stand-in Zotero, kept as `test/index.test.ts`.

- **What dies.** The reader `<browser>` of `reader.xhtml` is
  `type="content"` (line 343), so the internal reader and the iframe
  window live in a content compartment, nuked when the window closes. The
  reader object itself and its `_window` are chrome. The plugin sandbox is
  a system-principal `Cu.Sandbox` without `freshCompartment`
  (`xpcom/plugins.js` 137-160), so no wrapper between the sandbox and
  chrome objects is nuked. `Components.utils.isDeadWrapper(reader)` is
  therefore false for a gone reader. The redelivery walk (#38) asked
  exactly that, and never skipped one.
- **What throws.** Every read through `_internalReader` or
  `_iframeWindow`. The Proxy's fallthrough (`xpcom/reader.js` 76-89)
  throws as well, for any property the instance lacks (`tabID` on a
  `ReaderWindow`). `itemID`, a prototype getter over `_item`, reads fine.
  The six failed steps hit direct reads before any fallthrough: memory-sync's
  `_syncPersistedVoicesToManager`, highlight-style's views, the resume
  guard's `toggleReadAloudPaused`, and `_readAloudManager` in the live
  voice list and voice pick. A plain `{ _internalReader: <dead> }` fails
  the same six, with no Proxy. A dead `_iframeWindow` alone fails the
  shortcuts step, which listens for keys on it.
- **Who survived, and at what cost.** The walks of the plugin player,
  skipped lines, selection start, system-voice hiding, provider entries,
  speech text settings, the player voice list and the redelivery each
  catch per reader. They started, and each logged one `can't access dead
  object`; with the six failures, that made fifteen per start. Six
  diagnostics threw outright: `highlight`, `autoScroll`,
  `sentenceInView`, `skippedLines`, `textSettings` and `playerVoiceList`.
  `shutdown()` was unaffected.
- **The fix.** `readerGone` and `forEachReader` in
  `read-aloud/reader-access.ts`. A reader is skipped in silence when the
  reader object, its internal reader or its iframe window is dead. A throw
  on any other reader is logged, and the walk goes on. The fourteen
  startup walks go through `eachReader` in `src/index.ts`, and the
  redelivery walk's `isDead` is now `readerGone`. The six diagnostics
  give such a reader a `{ gone: true }` row. With one listed, the
  stand-in's start logs no dead-object error at all.
- **The stand-in Zotero.** Everything the test does not name answers an
  `anything` Proxy: never a thenable, iterating as empty, with
  `hasMoreElements()` false. The first run hung in `highlight levels`,
  because a `while (e.hasMoreElements())` over an `anything` never ends.
  The `strings` step needs a `Localization` with `formatValueSync` over
  the en-US file. Each start runs after `vi.resetModules()` and so gets
  its own `core/l10n`, which only that step fills. A test with no reader
  open pins that every step starts on the stand-in, so a step that fails
  there cannot pass the other checks vacuously.

## Verified live: a reinstall over a half-closed reader window starts every step, and Zotero's own session save trips on the entry (2026-09-25 13:44, issue #143)

Run on `1.14.4-beta7` (`9cc9d02`), macOS, Zotero 10.0.3. The table is
on #143's closing comment.

- **The stand-in's model holds.** After `reader._window.close()`, the
  entry stays listed with `_window.closed` true, and its
  `_internalReader` and `_iframeWindow` are both dead wrappers. That is
  what `test/index.test.ts` models.
- **The fix, live.** An in-place reinstall over the entry:
  - every step `ok` and the bare `started`, with no dead-object line from
    the plugin;
  - the six per-reader diagnostics give the entry `{ "gone": true }`;
  - the tab listed after it got its highlight patch and its live
    voice-list hook;
  - a tab opened after the reinstall got the Player through the
    `renderToolbar` listener, and Shift+Space opened the Player there.
- **Zotero's own costs of the entry remain.** Besides `Reader.open()`
  for that item (2026-09-24), Zotero's debounced session save logs `can't
  access dead object` at `chrome://zotero/content/xpcom/reader.js`, with
  no stack. It did so three times in eight minutes. Only a restart or a
  splice clears the entry.
- **Seen on the way, not investigated.** Closing the Player right after
  opening it on a fresh reader, before anything was read, threw twice in
  Zotero's own `_hideReadAloudJumpButton` (`this._readAloudJumpButton is
  undefined`, `reader.js:76908`). The stack runs from the plugin's close
  command through Zotero's `toggleReadAloudPopup` and the live voice
  list's hook on `deactivate`.

## A leftover `loadVoices` hook left the player empty, and the fix takes Zotero's methods from the class (2026-09-25 16:35, issue #131)

- **It is not only a console line.** In "My Vampire System — Chapters
  1–250", a tab open across the 1.14.4-beta7 install, the owner switched
  Fish Audio on as the only provider. The player's Provider, Language and
  Voice dropdowns stayed empty, and six `list is undefined` errors came in
  from 16:18 to 16:21. `liveVoiceList()` read `applied: 0` and
  `pluginPlayer()` read `providers: []`. After the tab was closed and
  reopened, the player showed Fish Audio, en-US and 9 voices, with no error.
  The tab's frozen list had held nothing the new settings allowed. #131
  went to `severity: major`.
- **The fix does not need to know where the first hook came from.** Zotero
  defines `loadVoices` and `deactivate` only on the manager's class. The
  live voice list now wraps the class's methods, so whatever sits on the
  manager itself is skipped. On shutdown it deletes its own property
  instead of restoring what it found there. A tab that already carries a
  leftover hook is repaired by the update itself, without reopening it.

## The paragraph pause is the whole pause, and neither Zotero number reaches the gap any more (2026-09-25 18:19, issue #142)

- **What changed.** `computeGap` waits one setting: the paragraph pause
  before a Segment anchored `paragraphStart`, the sentence pause elsewhere,
  each ÷ speed, and 0 where its switch is off. The sum of #44 is gone, and
  with it both fallbacks to Zotero's pacing: the voice's `sentenceDelay`
  (300 on Premium Voice 1-3, measured 2026-09-04) and `DELAY_PARAGRAPH`
  (200). The decision and what was turned down are ADR 0008.
- **Why the defaults sound the same.** Sentence on at 0 and paragraph on at
  200 gave 0 + 200 before a paragraph under the sum, and give 200 now. A
  pause changes only for a sentence pause other than 0, or a switch off.
- **What the voice's delay still does.** Only the Session's `catch` around
  `gapBefore` reads it, to wait Read Aloud's own sum if the settings
  cannot be read; the catalog value is otherwise inert in the Engine.
- **The phone.** xujialiu/OpenReader#60 (beta23) already read the
  paragraph pause as the whole pause; with this the same numbers give the
  same pauses on both devices.

## A rare skip at high speed made reproducible: the Engine's word clock replayed on measured delays (2026-09-25 19:59, issue #144)

- **Fish Audio times words in 80 ms steps.** Every start and end in its
  timings is a multiple of 0.08 s, so its short words ("the", "a", "at")
  are 80 ms long: 27 ms on screen at 3×, one or two frames at 60 Hz.
- **The reader window's timers while reading at 3×** (a `setTimeout(fn, 5)`
  chain, macOS, fixture-a.pdf): late by a median of 1 ms and p90 2 ms, but
  up to 35 ms. The idle max is 8 ms. The tail is what matters for words this
  short, and 39 words read live never met it. The run lost no word; the
  replay of #144 loses about 1 in 700.
- **`AudioContext.currentTime` moves in 128-frame quanta** (2.67 ms at
  48 kHz), and `outputLatency + baseLatency` held at 21.9 ms throughout.
  A probe that samples it in a loop must yield a macrotask between reads:
  a loop that only awaits a synchronous call (`diagnostics.engine()` is
  one) read the same value 7,000 times in a second, while playing.
- **Zotero draws every word change before the next frame.** The chain from
  the manager's `ActiveWordChange` listener to `Page.render` is a
  microtask and synchronous calls, with no throttle (the lines are on
  #144). A word the Engine emits is on screen. A word it never emits is
  what goes missing.
- **How the rare case became a loop.** `EngineSession` on the unit
  harness, fed the captured timings, with each timer delayed by a seeded
  draw from the 459 measured delays. It runs 19,500 words per speed in
  about a second and loses the same 80 ms words every run. Varying one
  input at a time settled both causes, the 15 ms floor on the next look
  and the delay tail (the table is on #144). The live side is a listener on
  `manager._controller` for `ActiveWordChange` and a `requestAnimationFrame`
  loop in the reader's iframe window, in the same clock. A minimized window
  stops the frames, so it stays shown while measuring.

## A word event is not a displayed frame, and the boundary retry is already adaptive (2026-09-25 20:12, issue #144)

Research only; the recommendation below is provisional, not an accepted
change. No production code, tests, or running Zotero were changed.

- **The present scheduler already targets the next boundary.**
  `src/core/engine/session.ts:689-703` reads the latency-adjusted audio
  clock, selects the latest due word, and sets one timer for the next
  word. Its 15 ms floor (`session.ts:64`) applies even when the next
  boundary is much closer. A callback that sees the quantized clock just
  before that boundary can therefore wait an unnecessary 15 ms.
  `src/core/engine/words.ts:40-53` intentionally selects only the latest
  due timing; it does not replay all elapsed words. The existing behavior
  is boundary scheduling with a minimum retry delay, not fixed polling.
- **Correction to the 19:59 entry.** An emitted word is not necessarily
  visible in a displayed frame. Zotero's copied manager listener reads
  the current index, and `_stateChanged` coalesces updates into one
  microtask (`test/fixtures/engine/read-aloud-manager-10.0.3.js:839-843`,
  `889-898`). Multiple word events in one task can therefore become one
  view update. Even separate tasks need not get separate frames: the
  [HTML event-loop standard][event-loop] explicitly permits coalescing
  timer callbacks without intermediate rendering. A synchronous page
  render proves an update was requested, not that the display presented
  that word. Event coverage, view-state coverage and visible frames are
  separate measurements.
- **The unit harness does not model either delay source.**
  `test/core/engine/harness.ts:41-57` executes callbacks at their due
  virtual time; its audio clock follows that clock continuously
  (`harness.ts:153-155`). The ordinary word tests
  (`test/core/engine/session.test.ts:499-590`) cover speed, latency,
  stalls and resume but neither quantized audio time nor missed frames.
  The issue's delayed replay supports the cause; it does not establish
  a production miss rate. In particular, the Engine's default clock uses
  sandbox-global timers (`src/read-aloud/engine/index.ts:142-145`), while
  the captured delay distribution came from reader-window timers.
- **A short boundary retry is the smallest candidate.** Keep the audio
  clock, output-latency correction and latest-due-word rule. Reduce the
  minimum retry near a boundary; retain a bounded wait when the audio
  clock does not advance so a stalled output cannot provoke a tight
  loop. The cost is additional callbacks close to word boundaries.
  The issue's 1 ms replay result is a partial improvement, not proof of
  zero skips. [HTML timers][timers] are not exact deadlines and nested
  web timers have a 4 ms minimum after five nestings; that is not a
  measurement of the plugin sandbox's timer implementation.
- **A frame callback is a competing design, not a guarantee.** Sampling
  the audio clock with `requestAnimationFrame` would align state updates
  with rendering opportunities, but refresh cadence then bounds the
  available samples. A missing frame can still cover an entire short
  word. Hidden documents are excluded from normal rendering updates by
  the [event-loop standard][event-loop], so a frame-only clock would need
  lifecycle and visibility handling. Compare actual visible-word coverage
  before accepting that extra mechanism.
- **Replaying or holding missed words changes the outcome.** Emitting
  every missed index together cannot force a frame for each. Giving each
  its own minimum visible duration instead makes the highlight lag the
  audio; under sustained overload that lag accumulates, or a catch-up
  rule must drop words anyway. These are product trade-offs, not a
  repair that preserves the accepted audio synchronization of ADR 0005.
- **What would distinguish a fix from an event-count improvement.** Use
  deterministic quantized-clock and delayed-callback cases, retaining
  latency, stall, resume and cancellation checks; then measure foreground
  PDF and EPUB frame coverage at high speed, including short words and
  deliberate main-thread delay. Count emitted words and frame-observed
  words separately. A frame observer is still a proxy for presentation,
  so do not claim a physical-display guarantee from it.

[event-loop]: https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/webappapis.html#event-loop-processing-model
[timers]: https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/timers-and-user-prompts.html#timers

## Short boundary retries follow audio time, with backoff only while that clock repeats (2026-09-25 21:17, issue #144)

- **Approved scope.** The owner accepted shorter boundary retries while
  preserving audio synchronization. Missed words are not replayed and no
  minimum visible duration is imposed. Long main-thread stalls can still
  skip words; the change does not promise zero omissions.
- **The change.** `EngineSession.wordTick` keeps its next-boundary timer
  and latest-due-word rule. The minimum wait is 1 ms after audio time
  moves; consecutive identical reads double it through 2, 4, 8 and 15 ms,
  capped there until audio time moves again. Each new source resets the
  backoff. This handles quantized time without making a suspended output
  spin at 1 ms near a boundary. The existing Engine diagnostic exposes
  `session.wordClock`: `ticks`, `shortWaits` (scheduled below 15 ms),
  `backoffs` and `lastWaitMs`, accumulated for that reading session.
- **Red then green.** With 128-frame quanta at 48 kHz, 21.9 ms latency
  and 80 ms words, the original code still has no word lit at 26 ms at
  each of 2×, 2.5× and 3×. The short retry passes those checks. Merely
  changing the minimum to 1 ms then fails the frozen-clock work bound:
  1,001 audio-clock reads over a second. Backoff passes the bound of fewer
  than 100 reads, including the fake output's end checks. Further cases
  keep only the current word after a 35 ms callback delay and verify
  cancellation on pause/end and fresh backoff on resume at another speed.
- **Verification boundary.** These are deterministic Engine tests, not
  display measurements. Engine case item 9 now requires separate event
  and frame-observed coverage in PDF and EPUB plus the retry-path
  diagnostic. Live verification is pending on `1.14.4-beta10`.
