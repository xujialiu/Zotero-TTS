## The docked bars lie over the document: the reader has two layers, and the player moved one (2026-09-24 00:06, issues #135, #137)

**Two layers over the document area.** `reader.html`'s body holds
`#reader-ui` and `#split-view` side by side. The document's views, their
iframes, are in `#split-view`. React renders into `#reader-ui`
(reader.js:83095), and that includes a second `.split-view` (42540) whose
`.primary-view` and `.secondary-view` hold the selection popup, the
annotation popup, the overlay popup and the find bar (42396–42437). One
rule places both layers, so they coincide: reader.css:1112,
`#split-view,.split-view{position:absolute;…;top:41px;bottom:var(--bottom-placeholder-height)}`.
`ViewPopup` (30747ff.) places a popup at the rect the view reports, in the
view's own coordinates, inside its parent on the popup layer, so a popup
sits by its text only while the two layers coincide. The find bar is
`.find-popup{position:absolute;z-index:70;…;inset-inline-end:15px;top:15px}`
(reader.css:284).

**What reserving space broke (#137).** Up to 1.14.4-beta2 an open docked
bar moved `#split-view` alone: `top: 75px !important` for the Top bar,
`bottom: 34px !important` for the Bottom bar. Measured 2026-09-23 on
Zotero 10.0.3-beta.3, with PDF and EPUB fixtures and the Top bar: the
document layer's top was at 75, the popup layer's at 41. The find bar
(56–128) lay 19 px under the player frame (41–75), 11 px of that the
search box, and `elementFromPoint` in that band answered the player
frame. A selection popup's top was 6.97 px below its word's top with the
player open, and 40.97 px with it closed: the word moved 34.00 px, and the
popup did not. The Bottom bar shifted nothing, because both layers' tops
stay at 41. The EPUB gave the PDF's numbers.

**What reserving space cost (#135).** A resized document area re-lays
the document out, and Zotero blurs an EPUB while it does: #124's
follow-up measured `blur(10px)` 100 ms after the container resized
(`mask-resizing`, reader.js:53571–53590 and 58078–58104; reader.css:1116–1124).
The owner saw a PDF blur at the player's opening too.

**Now.** Neither layer moves. The bars lie over the document's top or
bottom 34 px. While the Top bar shows, the find bar gets
`margin-top: 34px` in every view that reaches the top: the primary view,
and the secondary one side by side. Side by side is
`body.enable-vertical-split-view`; stacked is
`enable-horizontal-split-view`, whose lower view does not reach the top.
A margin adds to Zotero's own `top: 15px` rather than restating it.

**Follow under a bar.** `followTarget` takes an `inset`, the px covered at
the viewport's top and bottom. Fit, clipping, centering and the head's
place are measured against what the bar leaves uncovered, and the clamp
stays on the real scroll range. The visibility probes of the check after
a manual scroll (#100) shrink their viewport by the same inset. The
player computes the inset from its frame, which it finds by id
(`ztts-player-frame`) through the view iframe's `ownerDocument`, not
through its `entries` map. The follow reaches the view behind a waived
wrapper, and a Map keyed from the player's side misses it, the same trap
as `LAST` in sentence-in-view. The bar's band is 34 px from its frame's
edge, not the frame's box: with a menu open the frame grows to 460 px,
downward for the Top bar and upward for the Bottom bar. A paginated EPUB
gets no inset, because a page cannot scroll. Zotero keeps a
`--block-margin` of 40 px at a page's top and bottom, and 20 px once the
view is 800 px wide or narrower (reader.js:44911). A bar therefore covers
margin only on a wide page, and about 14 px of the first or last line on
a narrow one. That was accepted with the document's first and last
strips.

**For a later live run.** A DOM `Selection` on the PDF text layer raises
no selection popup on desktop, because `PDFNativeTextSelection` is
enabled only when `view._mobile` (reader.js:74760–74772).
`view._setSelectionRanges` (76691–76701), the call a mouse selection ends
in, does raise it. `drawSnapshot` renders the cross-process player iframe
black. The owner's profile still holds `local::af_fake` in
`readAloud.memory`, left by #133's second live run with the local
provider off. The owner chose to leave it, so a live run overrides it
before it opens a player.

## A native row's item was checked before the document analysis loaded and written after it, and the open's sync adopts in between (2026-09-24 01:25, issue #138)

Found by the position-sync audit with OpenReader at `cfe13e9`, reproduced in
a unit test with the real `createDocumentPositions` and
`createSharedTransport`; not seen live.

**The window.** `nameThenSyncOnOpen` (`src/index.ts`) names the EPUB, pokes
the Positions File's sync and runs `deriveSharedFromNative` beside it. The
derivation checked the held item against the native row, then awaited
`internal._loadSDT()`, then wrote. The await is short when Zotero already has
the document analysis's pack and takes as long as building it when it does
not, which is the case at a first open; the sync is one WebDAV round trip, so
at a first open it lands inside the await. #129 made that the moment a
phone's item for the book is first adopted.

**The wrong rule.** The write went through `recorded`, the sampler's path,
which stamps `max(at, held.stamp.at + 1)` (spec 6.6). That is right for a
sentence read now and wrong for any write carrying an old time: with the
phone's item at 5000 adopted during the await, the row's place from 1000 was
written at 5001 under this machine's id, uploaded by the next sync and
adopted by the phone.

**Who gets past the first check.** A row newer than the item held: one
brought from another computer through `zotero-tts-positions.json` (adopting a
row there never touches the Positions File's item), one from before 1.13.2,
one whose last sentences gave no capture. With one computer and ordinary
reading the item is written with every sentence and is never older than the
row.

**The fix.** `derived` in `document-positions.ts` writes at the row's own
time, only for a named attachment and only when that time is strictly newer
than the item held at the write; the check and the write are one synchronous
step, so nothing adopted before it is missed. `recorded` keeps the bump for
the sampler. The derivation logs `native row at <ts> derived | not derived
for <lib>/<key>; held: <device> at <at> | nothing`, which is what a live run
greps for. Spec 6.9 now says the same.

## A crafted native row needs Zotero's whole EPUB selector: without its `type` the mapper resolves nothing, and the derivation says nothing (2026-09-24 02:50, issue #138)

The first live run of the #138 fix saw no `native row at` line for either
fixture, and item 8's fixture never got an item. The rows it had crafted
into `zotero-tts-positions.json` carried `pos: {value: <cfi>}`, the shape
spec section 5 used to give. Zotero 10.0.3's `EPUBPositionMapper`
`sourceToSDTPosition` returns null for any position whose `type` is not
`'FragmentSelector'` (installed omni.ja, `resource/reader/reader.js`
62506-62509), so `deriveSharedFromNative` stopped at its silent
`if (!start || !end) return;`. The tester took the silence for a missing
`_internalReader` at `renderToolbar`, which it was not: item 8 passed on
2026-09-22 through the same open path. A real row is Zotero's
`readAloudState.savedPosition`, which `resolveSelectorMap` (62288 ff.) builds
as `{type: 'FragmentSelector', conformsTo, value}`; a crafted row copies that
object whole. The kit's harvest script had round-tripped the whole selector
and then kept only its `value`. Spec section 5 now shows the whole shape.

## A Positions File test leaves its fixtures' items in this machine's store, and the first sync after the owner's folder is restored uploads them (2026-09-24 05:38, issue #138)

Found by the second beta4 run. The owner's own `xujialiu-positions.json`
held the first beta4 run's crafted phone item for its `b` fixture
(`sha256:493a6223…`, `iPhone-test` at 1790187375546). The first run had
cleaned the test folder, then restored the owner's WebDAV address and turned

hundred bytes. But the Test WebDAV rule's "original local state restored"
cannot be met by a run that makes this machine adopt or derive an item:
nothing in the plugin forgets one.

## Two products writing back a carried item settle after one upload; the plugin's `null` for a missing field made an item adoptable (2026-09-24 05:56, issue #139)

Measured with both products' Positions File modules side by side (this repo
at `209d153`, OpenReader at `4d045e3`): 18 files as a third writer or a hand
edit might leave them, each parsed, merged and serialized by both, then synced
alternately from either side; no live run.

- **No re-upload loop.** Every case settled after one upload by the plugin,
  or two when OpenReader synced first and had to sort or de-duplicate.
  OpenReader re-emitted a carried item as parsed, so the plugin's canonical
  text was a fixed point for it, and neither product keeps a carried item in
  its store: the plugin's `local()` gives usable items only, OpenReader's
  `deps.local()` is its Library. The issue's first cost, an upload on every
  sync, did not hold.
- **`null` for a missing field changed more than bytes.** Both products refuse
  an item whose `publicationId` is neither a string nor `null`, so an `epub`
  item without the key is usable nowhere. The plugin's serializer wrote
  `"publicationId":null` into it, and after that one pass it was usable on
  both sides.
- **Two merge rules.** The plugin took an item's `stamp.at` whenever it was an
  integer; OpenReader's `parseStamp` also wants a non-empty `device`. With a
  local item at 5 against the file's at 9 with `device` `""`, OpenReader kept
  its own and the plugin the file's.
- **An off-grammar locator resolves in the plugin anyway.** `locatorPath`
  strips assertions, and `walkSnapshot`'s `related` takes a text step
  (`/4/2/1:0`) as inside its block by the path prefix, the next character
  being `/`. Refusing such an item in `usableItem` lost a place the anchor
  would have found.

What settles all four is spec 2.3, 2.5, 6.2, 6.4 and 6.7 as changed the same
day (section 7).

## Zotero's own player carries the media keys, and three ways the Player left it showing or showed nothing (2026-09-24 15:22, issue #134)

Read from the unpacked `omni.ja` of Zotero 10.0.3-beta.3 (BuildID
20260917121732) and from the plugin at `205aeab`; lines are the reader
bundle's, `resource/reader/reader.js`, unless marked.

**What Zotero's popup is.** `ReadAloudPopup` (38502-38666) is drawn while
`popupOpen && lang && !readAloudFirstRunPopup` (42595).

- **Media keys.** It alone registers the OS media keys and the now-playing
  session (`useMediaControls`, 38282-38415, called at 38557-38581). Play and
  pause go through a looping silent WAV `<audio>` (38233, 38310-38333);
  previous and next skip a paragraph.
- **No sample button.** `handleUserVoiceSelect` (38585-38593) plays a sample
  when a voice is picked in the popup while paused, and a resume stops it
  (38551-38556).
- **Everything a Zotero voice's account shows lives here and nowhere else.**
  The balance row (38918-38961) is hidden when the balance is unknown or
  over 90 days (38417-38421) and turns urgent under 3 minutes (82160,
  82254-82256). Each voice's time left sits in the list (38448-38481). The
  purchase link opens `https://www.zotero.org/settings/readaloud`
  (`xpcom/reader.js` 633-635). Signed out, a log-in row takes the tier
  picker's place (38863-38874). The options panel opens by itself on a
  quota or daily-limit error or a low balance (38546-38550). Errors offer
  Retry only (38962-38978). Settings → General → Read Aloud holds only
  *Highlight current* (`preferences/preferences_general.xhtml` 225-240).
  Now #140.
- **The balance keeps coming in without the popup.** `tts/credits` is
  polled every 60 s while a controller exists (82641-82646), drawn or not,
  and every `tts/voices` answer carries the balance in headers
  (`xpcom/sync/syncAPIClient.js` 620-650).

**The dialogs with samples.** The first-run voice dialog opens only from
`toggleReadAloudPopup(true)` while `reader.readAloudVoices` is empty
(84200-84203 → 83390-83399 → `xpcom/reader.js` 640-643).
`startReadAloudAtPosition` (84239-84266) never raises it, and neither does
the Player's button, which starts through `smartPlay`. Its buy link is dead:
no `reader.ftl` has the `<purchase-credits>` markup it needs (39217), and the
handler is a TODO (`readAloudFirstRunDialog.js` 38-40). The Manage Voices
dialog is unreachable: the bundle never calls `onOpenReadAloudVoicesPopup`
(`xpcom/reader.js` 644-647).

**Entry points.**

- The toolbar button calls `toggleReadAloudPopup` (83123, 84193-84219).
- Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+R and Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+L (81976-81983) close when reading
  is active with nothing selected, and otherwise call
  `startReadAloudAtPosition`. *Read Aloud from Here* (80874-80878) calls it
  too.
- The tab's icon only pauses (`tabs.js` 301-304).
- No menu item exists.
- H and U annotate the sentence while Read Aloud is active (82110-82116),
  but not from a focused button or select.
- A reader window has all of it: `ReaderWindow` extends `ReaderInstance`
  (`xpcom/reader.js` 2204), and `_open` passes `enableReadAloud: true` (264).

**How the Player failed before #134.**

- *A frame that never finished loading* left Zotero's popup hidden and an
  empty transparent frame. The toolbar button still started a reading, with
  no controls, and only "player did not finish loading" reached the log.
- *An `attach` that threw before its first paint* never wrote the hiding
  rule, since `paint` wrote it, so that reader showed Zotero's popup.
- *At an upgrade* the old instance removed the rule first, so Zotero's popup
  showed until the successor attached. The gap can include the shutdown's
  bounded flushes, 8 s each. The successor then started closed over the
  still-open session: it opens only on a change of Zotero's open state, so
  nothing was on screen until the button was clicked, and the click resumed
  the reading.
- *Shift+O and the `playerOptions` diagnostic* fell back to the hidden
  popup's Options button while the Player was closed, which clicked a
  button no one could see.

ADR 0007 records what #134 does about each: the rule goes in first, a failed
reader refuses a reading with a message, and an upgrade keeps the rule. The
successor still starts closed, by the owner's choice, and Shift+O now falls
through.

## Verified live: Zotero's own player never shows, even across an update, and a half-closed reader window fails six startup steps (2026-09-24 16:46, issue #134)

Run on `1.14.4-beta6` (`2aa927e`), macOS, Zotero 10.0.3-beta.3; the table
is on #134's closing comment.

- **An in-place reinstall mid-reading.** `.read-aloud-popup`'s computed
  `display` read `none` on all 210 samples, 50 ms apart over 12.7 s. The
  new instance's Player resource answered 10.7 s after the install began.
  The successor's Player was closed, the Engine had adopted the paused
  session (`stats.adopted` 1), and the button resumed the same segment.
- **The Player's own failure, its resource unregistered.** The reader
  reported `failed: "frame"`, the toast came on the button and on
  Shift+Space, and the popup never showed. With the window in the
  background and the bridge busy, both the 5 s give-up and the 250 ms
  check ran well late; the order of events held.
- **A reader window closes through Zotero's `reader.close()`.** The
  window's `onclose` and `cmd_close` both call it (`reader.xhtml` 21, 52).
  It runs `uninit()`, `window.close()` and the `onClose` that takes the
  reader out of `Zotero.Reader._readers` (`xpcom/reader.js` 2254-2258,
  2960-2963). A script's `reader._window.close()` skips all three and
  leaves a dead entry. Zotero's own `Reader.open()` then trips on it for
  that item. At the next reinstall, six of the plugin's startup steps
  fail on it: Read Aloud memory, highlight colors, sentence in view, live
  voice choices, voice switching and Read Aloud shortcuts. Each walks
  `_readers` with no guard per reader; the `plugin player` step guards
  each. No user path leaves such an entry, and removing it and
  reinstalling started every step.
