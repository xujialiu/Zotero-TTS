## The highlight levels are the plugin's own two switches: Zotero's pref pinned to them through its own observers, and its settings menulist greyed (2026-09-16 00:20, issue #114)

Built on `feat/highlight-levels` as 1.12.11-beta3, verification pending.
Settled with the owner in a grilling session on 2026-09-15, one decision
per round: the control goes into the settings pane only, since the player
is to be rebuilt later and nothing new goes into Zotero's popup; the value
is the plugin's own and Zotero's Highlight current stops being a choice;
two levels as two checkboxes, Sentence and Word, both on by default and
never both off; no paragraph; everyone starts with both on at the upgrade
(no migration from Zotero's level or the old switch); Zotero's menulist
greyed with a hint rather than hidden; each switch on its color's row,
Sentence above Word.

**The pin, and why it is the whole mechanism.** Zotero still draws the
highlight, and the plan's first idea — apply the level per reader through
`setReadAloudHighlightGranularity` (`reader.js:83948`) and leave Zotero's
pref alone — fails three ways: Zotero's per-reader observer
(`xpcom/reader.js:671` → `_handleReadAloudHighlightGranularityChange`,
`:1241`) writes the pref's value back into the state on every change of
that pref; a tab opened later takes the pref at construction
(`xpcom/reader.js:268` → the bundle's initial state, `reader.js:82990`);
and pref observers fire in registration order, so a re-apply from the
plugin's observer is undone by the observer of any reader opened after
the plugin registered (memory-sync's pitfall). So `core/highlight-pin.ts`
keeps the pref itself equal to `word` while the Word switch is on and
`sentence` otherwise: written at start, on either switch's change (an
observer per switch pref), and written back *inside its own observer*
whenever anything else writes it. A foreign write then reaches the
readers registered before the pin with the foreign value first and, in
the nested notification the write-back raises, with the plugin's; the
readers registered after the pin read the pref when their turn comes and
see the plugin's value only — both end where the switches say. The
write-back cannot loop (inside it the value already agrees, and a `set`
of an unchanged value notifies nobody). Not one reader method is hooked,
and every one of Zotero's own paths — the observer's repaint inside the
write, measured for #67, and the new tab's construction — carries the
plugin's value. At shutdown the observers come off and the pref stays
where the plugin left it: Zotero alone then highlights as last set, and
its menulist works again.

**Zotero's settings window.** The menulist is
`#read-aloud-highlight-granularity-menulist`
(`preferences_general.xhtml:230`, 10.0.3-beta.1), a `preference=`-bound
XUL menulist. Zotero loads each pane lazily (`preferences.js:299`,
`_loadPane`: a sandboxed script, then the markup appended to the pane's
container, `document.l10n.translateFragment`, `_initImportedNodesPostInsert`,
`pane.loaded = true`) and announces nothing — no event, no observer
notification — so `ui/zotero-highlight-menu.ts` takes every settings
window (`Services.wm.getEnumerator('zotero:pref')` for the open ones,
`Services.ww.registerNotification`'s `domwindowopened` plus the window's
`load` for the rest, the `windowtype` read after the load since an
opening window has no document yet) and, when the menulist is not there,
watches the document with a MutationObserver until it is, then sets
`disabled="true"` and a `tooltiptext` (`ztts-zotero-highlight-hint`) and
disconnects; the original attributes go back at shutdown on every window
still alive. A fact found on the way: a plugin pane's `stylesheets` are
inserted as document-wide `xml-stylesheet` processing instructions
(`preferences.js:323-328`), so a rule for Zotero's own pane *would* apply
from the plugin's registration — but only once the plugin's pane has
loaded in that window, which is why CSS alone could not grey the General
pane.

**The pane and the key.** `ui/highlight-rows.ts` greys the color and
opacity of a level that is off and the last switch still on (a
`preference=`-bound checkbox with `disabled="true"`), repaints the
one-line preview from the switches, and watches the two switch prefs so
a change made behind the pane's back — `Shift+W`, a restore, the sync —
regreys and repaints; both prefs `false` (a hand-edited profile) is read
as the sentence everywhere (`settleLevels`) and, with the pane open,
written back on. `Shift+W` flips the Word switch and, if that would leave
nothing, turns Sentence on in the same press (`toggleWord`); its toast
names what is highlighted now (`Highlight: word and sentence` /
`sentence` / `word`, the no-timing long form whenever Word is on and the
timing is the stand-in). `highlight-style.ts` lost nothing: its
`style.sentenceUnderWord` became `style.sentence`, the sentence under the
word drawn as before; with Word off Zotero itself is at `sentence`. The
old pref `highlight.sentenceUnderWord` is cleared at startup.

**Diagnostics.** `diagnostics.highlightLevels()`: the switches, the pin
(`wanted`, `zotero`, `pinned`, `snapped` — foreign writes undone since
start), the menu (per settings window `found` / `disabled`), and per
reader the state's `highlightGranularity` with the word timing;
`highlightKey(press)` reports `switches` and `levels` instead of the
single level. The startup step is `highlight levels`, right before
`highlight colors`.

**Left for the run.** Whether a Sentence-switch change while paused waits
for the resume, as the old switch did (checklist 5.5), and how a settings
window open across the in-place install behaves. The README's Highlight
screenshot shows the old rows, like #78's shortcuts one.

## The OpenAI section split into three, and the old keys read once (2026-09-16 00:40, issue #113)

Until 1.12.11 one section, `openai`, held four servers behind a Server
dropdown: `openai.server` named the one in force, the section's five
fields held its values, and `openai.presetValues` remembered the other
three (issues #34, #52, #54 were all the memory's). Issue #113 makes three
providers of it — `openai-official` (api.openai.com, fixed, key required),
`mimo` (api.xiaomimimo.com, fixed, key required, the chat completions
route of #50) and `compatible` (any address, key optional, gateway
headers) — sharing one client, `core/providers/openai-compatible.ts`,
which is the old `openai.ts` with its hostname sniffing replaced by what
the caller says: whether a key is required, the route, the documented
voices, the label its errors carry. Chatterbox-TTS-Server gets no
section: probed live on 2026-09-15 (the issue's table), it works through
the generic path with nothing of its own — address and gateway headers,
key empty, any model, the server's 28 voices from `/v1/audio/voices`,
`/v1/models` a 404 the client tolerates.

**Why the OpenAI section's id is `openai-official`, not `openai`.** The
plan kept `openai`. But `openai.apiKey` means two things across the split:
to a copy still on ≤1.12.11 it is the key of whichever server the dropdown
has in force, to the new version it would be the OpenAI section's. Through
the WebDAV settings sync (#68) the two meanings would cross during the
transition window — a MiMo key on the old copy overwritten by the OpenAI
section's key from the new one, and the reverse — and a new-version
backup restored into an old version would do the same. With a fresh id the
old keys are exactly `openai.*`, no new key overlaps one, and the old
copies ignore everything the new one writes. Persisted keys never follow a
display rename, but this was a change of meaning, not of display.

**The conversion is one module, `core/openai-split.ts`, deleted in
2.0.0** (the owner's rule, as #22 for the position store; the chore that
lists what goes is opened when #113 ships). Three entry points, each a
line at its caller: at startup after `migrateLegacyProviderPref`, the old
prefs — any `openai.*` with a value — become the three sections' prefs, the
remembered voices are re-prefixed (Zotero's `reader.readAloudVoices`
entries' `voice` and `tierVoices`, the key `openai` moved to the target
tier in the same position since the last key is Zotero's fallback; the
plugin's `readAloud.memory`; `readAloud.favoriteVoices`) and the old prefs
cleared, so it runs once; in `parseBackup`, before the known-key filter,
a file's `openai.*` keys become the three sections' keys where the file
does not already hold them; in `parseSharedSettings`, a file's `openai.*`
items give the three sections' items — the section in force field by
field from the field's own item, all three switches from the switch item,
the remembered sections from the memory item — each with the `ts` and
`by` of the item it came from, nothing derived from an item the file
lacks, the old items left in the file for the old copies, and the newer
of a derived item and one already there under the new key winning. The
Chatterbox and Other sets compete for the one compatible slot: the one in
force first, else Chatterbox, else Other.

**`heldSections` and an empty address.** `isLocalAddress('')` is true (an
address that does not parse is local, on purpose), and `compatible.baseURL`
starts empty, so the sync would have held the compatible section on every
fresh machine forever — a public compatible server configured on one
computer could never reach another. An empty address now holds nothing:
there is no server to be local. `local.baseURL` defaults to localhost and
stays held, as the owner's 2026-09-10 rule wants.

**Seen while probing.** With a provider off, Test connection ends by
unlocking the section's fields, and the unlock re-rendered the old Server
dropdown from the prefs (`onUnlocked` → `presetRows.refresh()`): a probe
that wrote `openai.server` and restored it afterwards left the dropdown
showing the probe's value, since the pref write did not re-render the
unbound menu. Gone with the dropdown; worth remembering for any other
unbound row.

`diagnostics.openaiSplit()` (synchronous) reports this start's split — the
target, the three switches, how many voice prefs were rewritten, how many
old keys cleared — the three sections with keys and headers by length, and
any old key still holding a value, which must be none.

## Verified live, issue #114: the pin snaps inside the write, an in-place reinstall keeps the previous prefs.js default, and a bare W on the library pane is Zotero's (2026-09-16 01:20, issue #114)

The tester's run of 1.12.11-beta3 on Zotero 10.0.3-beta.1 (Windows),
`cases/highlight-levels.md` items 1–11, `word-highlight-key.md` 4.10
and `highlight.md` 5.5 / 3.5: every item PASS, every touched pref back
byte for byte, the error console clean. The kit is
`test/zotero-dev/scripts/highlight-levels/` (18 scripts) and the two
older kits point at it.

- **The pin behaves as designed.** A foreign write of `paragraph` or
  `sentence` to Zotero's pref reads back `word` in the statement after
  the write — the snap lands inside the write, `pin.snapped` counting
  each — and every open reader's state stays `word`; a write of the
  pinned value itself changes nothing and counts nothing; with Word off
  a foreign `word` snaps to `sentence`. The Word switch flips the PDF's
  primary from a word rect to the segment's rects inside the write,
  playing and paused, `_position` and the controller unchanged; the
  Sentence switch flips `style.sentence` at once and the drawn pieces at
  the next state push (350 ms later playing; while paused, only a push —
  `manager.repositionTo(sameIndex)` counts as one — or the resume), which
  is checklist 5.5's rule unchanged.
- **Zotero's menulist.** A settings window opened on the plugin's own
  pane reads `{found: false}` until General is shown, then `{found:
  true, disabled: true}` — the MutationObserver route works; a window
  open across the in-place install was greyed by the new instance; a
  pick forced through the binding (`menulist.value = 'sentence'` plus
  `command`) is written back inside the write and the menulist re-reads
  `word`. After a plugin reload the resident window is greyed again.
- **An in-place reinstall keeps the previous build's `prefs.js`
  defaults.** `highlight.sentenceUnderWord` no longer declared and its
  user value cleared at startup still *reads* `true`:
  `Services.prefs.getDefaultBranch('').getBoolPref` holds the default the
  beta2 `prefs.js` registered, `prefHasUserValue` is `false`, and only a
  Zotero restart re-parses every plugin's defaults. The load-bearing
  claim for a retired pref across an upgrade is therefore "no user
  value", never `undefined`; the case files say so now.
- **The library pane consumes a bare W.** A trusted Shift+W with the
  library tab selected and nothing speaking left the switches, the pref
  and every document untouched, but `tip.keydown()` returned `2` where
  the 2026-09-08 run saw `0` — Zotero's own item-tree key handling, most
  likely the quick search, not the plugin's; the case reads "not `1`"
  now.
- **Two environment findings, not the plugin's.** `manager.selectVoice()`
  called from chrome never moved `selectedVoiceID` (string id or voice
  object, paused or playing, no throw); the route that works is Zotero's
  own persisted-voice restore — close the popup, repoint the language's
  entry in `reader.readAloudVoices`, reopen, and re-read the manager,
  which is a new instance. The return-key EPUB's AudioContext stayed
  `suspended` after a trusted Shift+Space restart (the "prevented from
  starting automatically" / `NotAllowedError` pair in the errors) where
  fixture A's identical restart ran, so the EPUB's live word advance was
  not testable this run; its switches, pref, DOM and spotlight colors
  were, read through `_getSpotlightColor`.
- **The rect figures are the segment's.** 4.10's 14–20 pt word against
  106–361 pt segment became 18–54 against 150 and wider at another
  position; the cases cite them as a narrow rect against wide ones now.
- Item 4.10's "the next segment draws at the new level" after the end
  of a document was not re-read this run (the script's own cleanup
  restored Word first); the same mechanism is established by every
  other press, and the kit notes it.

## The split ran twice: Gecko keeps a gone prefs.js's defaults, and the recovery (2026-09-16 03:10, issue #113)

**Incident.** The tester's first run of #113's build installed 1.12.12-beta
in place over 1.12.11-beta3. The migration ran right — the MiMo key (51
chars) into `mimo.apiKey`, the Chatterbox set into `compatible.*`, the
nine `openai.*` user values cleared. The case then asked for a second
in-place install of the same xpi to prove the migration does not run
twice. It ran twice: target `openai-official`, `openai-official.enabled`
true with no key, `mimo.apiKey` and `compatible.baseURL` / `headers` /
`model` blanked — the owner's MiMo key and Chatterbox gateway token wiped
from the live profile; and the settings auto-upload, switched back on at
the run's end, pushed the wiped state to this machine's WebDAV file.

**Why.** Zotero registers a plugin's `prefs.js` on the default branch at
every load (`chrome/content/zotero/xpcom/plugins.js:507`,
`setDefaultPrefs`, `ignoreCache: true`) and at the plugin's shutdown runs
`clearDefaultPrefs` (543), which calls `clearUserPref` on the default
branch for each pref of the same file — and `clearUserPref` on the default
branch does not remove a default (measured live: `openai.voice`, no user
value, stays type 32 with the value `alloy` after it). So the defaults of
every build loaded in a Zotero process last until Zotero quits: after the
in-place upgrade, `openai.enabled` true, `openai.baseURL`, `openai.model`,
`openai.voice` and the blank defaults of the other five were still
readable through `Zotero.Prefs.get(fullName, true)`, and `Zotero.Prefs.clear`
only removes the user value, so the migration's gate — "any `openai.*`
pref reads a non-empty value" — reopened at the next install and split the
defaults over the sections it had just filled. The tester's post-install
script had read the nine as `undefined` with `Zotero.Prefs.get('zotero-tts.openai.x', true)`:
a relative name with the global flag reads `undefined` (the rulebook's own
pitfall), so the defaults were there after the first migration too.

**The fix** (commit after 3c40365): `PrefsBackend.has(key)` =
`Zotero.Prefs.prefHasUserValue(key, true)`; the gate and the clearing
count user values only (`legacyPrefSet`), and `diagnostics.openaiSplit()`
lists `staleDefaults` — the old prefs whose default is still registered,
all nine after an in-place upgrade from ≤1.12.11, none after a full
restart — beside `legacyPrefs`, which must be empty. A migration's "done"
test is `prefHasUserValue`, never a read: an in-place upgrade is not a
fresh process.

**Recovery.** The macOS copy's settings file on WebDAV
(`zotero-tts-settings_macos.json`, 2026-09-14, 1.12.9-beta) still held the
old section: `openai.apiKey` of length 51 with `openai.server` mimo — the
MiMo key — and the Chatterbox set's 151-character headers in
`openai.presetValues`. A script inside Zotero copied them into
`mimo.apiKey` and `compatible.headers` without either value leaving the
sandbox; the headers equal `local.headers` and `fishspeech.headers` byte
for byte (one Cloudflare Access token for the h200 hosts), the Chatterbox
address and `tts-1` were written by hand from the 2026-09-15 probe, and
`openai-official.enabled` went back to false. Nothing was lost. This
machine's own file had already been overwritten; the older unsuffixed
file and `_xujialiu-win11` predate MiMo.

## The re-run passes, and three things seen on the way (2026-09-16 10:40, issue #113)

The fixed build (7f7d953, `1.12.12-beta`, xpi
`f0a50c006f09dfcb787cf40ebac1885031b9f5411ba15dae2d4d6da73dc9ca73`) on the
owner's profile: every item of `cases/openai-split.md` PASS — the
migration stayed silent through two in-place installs with all nine
`staleDefaults` present and the sections byte-identical; MiMo's probe
went through `/v1/models` then `/v1/chat/completions` with the bearer and
no gateway header, the Chatterbox's through `/v1/audio/speech` with the
gateway headers and no bearer; both sections on at once, two entries in
the player and the browser, a sentence each (`mimo: no word timestamps …
(audio through /v1/chat/completions)`, `compatible: no word timestamps
…`). The table is on the issue.

- **Zotero's own persistence drifts `reader.readAloudVoices` during a
  run.** Every voice pick of item 4 rewrote the `mul` entry through
  `_persistCurrentVoice` (1397 → 1465 characters); the kit's cleanup now
  restores the pref byte for byte from the pre-run read, and the case
  says so.
- **Four `can't access dead object` errors from the bundle** at the
  fixture's close-then-erase (`toggleReadAloudPopup(false)`, `eraseTx()`
  some 300 ms later), two offsets twice each, no in-place install
  involved — not #5's pattern. The position tracking's `tab.onClose` and
  `delete` notifier hooks (`src/index.ts`) are the plausible seat; not
  dug into, every check completed. Proposed to the owner as an issue of
  its own.
- **A rule changed mid-run is not a rule to the tester.** The owner's
  permission to close their player was written into the rulebook and
  sent to the running tester as a message; the tester had read the
  rulebook before the edit, found the message contradicting both the
  file it remembered and its brief, and declined it as an injection —
  rightly, by its rules. A rule change reaches a run through its brief,
  or through the next run.
