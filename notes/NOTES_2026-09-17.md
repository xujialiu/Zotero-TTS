## Playback preparation ends at source start, not buffering completion (2026-09-17 13:46, issue #120)

The owner requested a preparation notice for initial playback, resume,
navigation, and sentence-to-sentence audio waits. It appears only after
300 ms and disappears immediately when playback starts. Intentional sentence
pauses do not count toward that wait. Failure replaces it; pause, stop, and
player closure clear it. A pending voice-switch notice takes priority and
retains the lifetime settled in #119.

The installed reader bundle's `_speakInternal` clears `buffering` and emits
`ActiveSegmentChange` before calling `_playAudioBuffer` (40162-40220).
`_playAudioBuffer` sets `_isPlaying` before `source.start`, so a flag alone
also cannot prove successful startup (40019-40051). The observer waits for
that native call to return successfully and for its output context to be
running; suspended output retains the notice until its state changes.
Manager activation includes the period before document segments and a
controller exist (82550-82568). `_scheduleSpeak` calls `_speak` only after
the configured gap (39404); observing that boundary avoids duplicating the
pause settings or starting a preparation timer during intentional silence.

`read-aloud/playback-notice.ts` observes only the manager's current controller.
Native prefetch and #119's unadopted prepared controller do not request ordinary
notices. Prototype hooks stay underneath the switcher's temporary instance
hooks, so restoring a one-shot handoff hook cannot erase the observer.
Controller errors are observed independently of the native error string:
decode/start failures can emit Error with no meaningful message. Explicit
pause/stop cancels notice eligibility before late errors arrive. Hooks,
listeners and delayed display work are removed when a reader detaches.

`ui/voice-notice.ts` arbitrates the ordinary and switching notices and keeps
short speed/status toasts independent. Diagnostics expose the observer phase,
whether preparation has requested an overlay, and native source-start counts;
the DOM remains the evidence of actual visibility when a switch suppresses
an ordinary notice. Automated regressions cover the threshold, source-start
gap, suspended output, cancellation, errors, stale/background controllers,
intentional pauses, and priority. Live verification is tracked on #120.

## Settings changes protect the affected reading sessions (2026-09-17 17:23, issue #121)

The guard now evaluates proposed settings before writes. The shared impact
check protects every open session, paused and background readers included,
and both voices of a prepared handoff. A used provider's configuration and
removal of a current voice are refused. Unused providers and unrelated
favorites may change. A restore is checked as one batch; background sync
keeps provider sections and the favorites pair together, applies unaffected
changes, and defers affected groups. Playback-related settings such as volume
are protected against incidental changes from a restore/sync; direct playback
controls remain intentional actions. A provider check that returns after a
session starts cannot disable its provider.

The custom player still uses Zotero's engine. Native `loadVoices`
(`resource/reader/reader.js`, installed reader bundle, 82310–82322) needs only
`_options` and two completion callbacks before assigning `_allVoices` and
`_devMode`. The live-list adapter invokes that native loader on a separate
reader-compartment object, then publishes the fresh choices. An active
manager is never re-resolved: its controller, current voice objects and
catalog array identity are retained, including both sides of a prepared
handoff (voice-switch validates that catalog identity). Missing protected
voices are retained through a failed or incomplete listing. An idle manager
still takes the normal resolution path. Revision checks discard results
superseded by another settings edit, player close or teardown. A settings
edit also clears a closed manager's cached list and voice: a warm reopen
otherwise resolves that stale list before fresh discovery lands, and could
start a provider disabled while the player was closed.

The remote-interface callbacks also matter: `memory-sync.reconcile` normally
stages a remembered choice against the freshly offered list. During an active
session it now updates the published inventory without changing the choice;
otherwise a failed listing could alter shared memory before the safe list
publication. Preference observers coalesce a batch of list changes and
refresh all open readers; edits to disabled providers and unfiltered favorite
marks do not fetch lists. Ending a session pokes deferred settings sync even
when the reader tab stays open.

Verification is pending on 1.12.12-beta6. Diagnostics `readingImpact` report
only titles and voice/provider IDs, never the proposed configuration values;
`liveVoiceList` reports applied/loading/retained counts and revision.
