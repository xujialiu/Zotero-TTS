## An expanded layout transition reports the old bar height (2026-09-18 00:22, issue #124)

Research on installed 1.12.12-beta8 found the owner's paused EPUB player
in layout B with a 300 × 34 iframe around 300 × 202 content. The title
row remained visible; the transport started below the frame at y=41.
The complete evidence and source references are on [#124](https://github.com/xujialiu/Zotero-TTS/issues/124).

An isolated iframe loaded the installed resources and reproduced expanded
A → B three times, without touching the reading session. The child changed
its variant before `render()` closed the old popover. That close reported
the old layout-A DOM's 34px height before the new DOM replaced it.
`updateControls()` only reports height when the collapsed class changes;
the expanded panel never takes that branch. Waiting or sending an unchanged
snapshot did not correct it. The collapsed comparison did take the branch
and reported 108 after the stale 34. Notifying against the completed
floating DOM correctly measured 202.

This differs from the stale resource cache incident of September 16:
the installed resource has the expected controls and dimensions, but the
transition reports them in the wrong order. A layout identifier matching
the saved preference does not prove that its content fits the frame. The
position shortcut's previous live checks covered state preservation and
layout identifiers, leaving this expanded geometry condition uncovered.

Research probes are in `.tmp/zotero-dev/floating-height/`; they call the
real child renderer, not the keyboard listener. The keyboard trigger is
the owner's report and its route is confirmed in source. No production
fix was made. The recommendation is to report the completed layout's size
after rendering and avoid reporting the outgoing DOM during replacement;
implementation awaits the owner's decision.

Temporary frames were removed; no preferences, playback state, providers,
or installation were changed. Geometry required the already visible host;
it was minimized after research (`windowState=2`). The latest five console
entries predated the probes and contained no plugin error.

## EPUB resize deliberately blurs the reading view (2026-09-18 01:05, issue #124)

The owner reported a brief blur for Bottom → Floating and Floating → Top,
but not Top → Bottom. Source research in the installed Zotero 10.0.3
bundle found an explicit EPUB resize mask: `resource/reader/reader.js`
53571–53590 observes container dimensions; EPUB leading/trailing resize at
58078–58104 adds `mask-resizing` and clears it after a 250ms delay.
`resource/reader/reader.css` 1116–1124 gives that class `filter: blur(10px)`.

The plugin reserves 34px for a docked player and none for floating.
Moving between the two docked positions preserves net viewport height,
whereas entering/leaving floating changes it by 34px. This explains the
reported direction dependence without a reader reload. The player normally
reuses its existing iframe document and replaces its controls in place;
only an unavailable child layout bridge takes the src fallback.

This is source evidence, with a live class/filter and load-event check
pending. No change to Zotero's resize mask has been selected. Removing
the mask alone would expose the intermediate EPUB reflow; reserving docked
space for floating would waste reading area. The current recommendation
is to retain Zotero's transition behavior.

## Anything on the player's render path runs at frame rate (2026-09-18 23:15, issue #125)

With the Read Aloud player open, scrolling this profile's EPUB ran at
12.8 fps against 114.4 fps with it closed, 91% of frames over 50 ms. The
cost was not the compositor: a 0 ms timer chain measured during the same
scroll returned a median delay of 82.7 ms against 8.8 ms, matching the
83.3 ms frame gap. That timer-chain check is the cheap way to tell a
blocked main thread from paint, and it is what turned the investigation
around after four wrong hypotheses had been measured and killed.

Zotero reschedules an open popup on every scroll event: `_handleScroll`
(reader.js 55160) puts `_renderAnnotations()` and `_repositionPopups()`
(53973) in a `requestAnimationFrame`. Wrapping both showed exactly 130
calls over a 130-frame pass — one per frame. Repositioning re-renders the
popup, and `TierSelect` builds its element at render time, so
provider-tiers.ts's wrapper on `React.createElement` — which sits in front
of every element the reader creates — reached `scan()` once per frame.
**So anything behind that wrapper's guard runs at the display's frame
rate, not once per popup open.** `scan()` called the injected `tierOf`
once per voice, and that dependency was
`pluginVoiceTier(id, providerNaming(loadSettings(prefs)).localEngine)` —
a full `loadSettings` over 66 preferences per voice. At 1,819 listed
voices and a measured 0.0005 ms per `Zotero.Prefs.get`, one walk is
≈120,000 reads ≈ 60 ms, which is the 83 ms frame. The dependency is now a
factory the walk opens once (`voiceTiers()`), in provider-tiers.ts and in
live-voice-list.ts, which had the same per-voice call.

Measuring this needs care. `requestAnimationFrame` in the reader stops
while the Zotero window is minimized, so frame-rate passes need a restored
window and a minimized one silently returns zero frames. `windowUtils` is
undefined on a content window reached through a waived view, so
`paintCount` is not available that way. `Services.profiler` returns the
**raw** profile format (v31): its `stackTable`/`frameTable`/`samples` are
`{schema, data}` pairs with no `funcTable` at all and a plain
`stringTable` array, so a leaf is
`stringTable[frameTable.data[stackTable.data[s][schema.frame]][schema.location]]`.
With `js,stackwalk,cpu` on GeckoMain it put 95.1% of samples inside
Zotero-TTS and named the stack outright.

Two things that look alarming here and are not ours: `reader.css:1116`
gives every view iframe a permanent `filter: blur(0px)` with a
`transition`, so it can animate to `blur(10px)` under `.mask-resizing`
(issue #124's masking) — overriding it to `none` changed nothing. And the
plugin's own player overlay is not involved either: with
`readAloud.usePluginPlayer` false the scroll still ran at 12.9 fps. The
attribution that settled it was disabling Zotero-TTS altogether, which put
an active session back to 111 fps with no long frames.
