## Zotero NFC-normalizes an EPUB's text, and the highlight patch's === check goes dark on a document stored decomposed (2026-09-08 00:52, issue #74)

**The follow-up to issue #73.** The reporter answered that Alina and
Emil highlight by sentence too, and that the sentence highlight misses
the start or the end of what is read, in EPUB and PDF. A second bridge
run, on Romanian text with ă/ș/ț in three encodings — comma-below
precomposed, cedilla precomposed, NFD — as an EPUB zipped by Python and
a PDF printed by Chrome headless (`--headless=new
--no-pdf-header-footer --print-to-pdf`), Azure-Alina, highlight at word:

- Every segment of both files got N word timestamps = its word count;
  no word was dropped in any encoding. Azure echoes back the exact form
  it was sent (the spans hold `foloseşte`, `ţăranii` verbatim on the
  cedilla paragraph, the decomposed forms on the PDF), so
  `alignWordsToText`'s exact search always matches. The diacritics
  hypothesis is dead.
- The sentence highlight covered the spoken segment exactly in all 26
  segments: in the EPUB head + word + tail reconstruct the segment; in
  the PDF the sentence rects equal `activeSegment.sourcePosition.rects`,
  multi-line sentences included, and with a wordless voice's stand-in
  too. The reporter's "beginning or end not highlighted" did not
  reproduce. Untested ground: inline elements splitting a paragraph's
  text nodes (issues #3/#4), soft hyphens and NBSP, a sentence across
  two blocks. It needs the reporter's file.
- Zotero's highlight default is `sentence` (`defaults/preferences/
  zotero.js` 1814). The reporter almost certainly never changed it,
  which is the whole of "every voice highlights the sentence".

**What Zotero does with text forms.** An EPUB's text reaches Read Aloud
NFC-normalized: reader.js 62066–62074 (DeltaMap, "position shifts
between NFC-normalized text and the original DOM text … origPos =
nfcPos − getDelta(deltaMap, nfcPos)") and the SDT text-node comment at
63023 ff.; `addSegment` (71223) only collapses whitespace
(`normalizeText`, 71709). `toDisplayedRange()` returns the document's
own form. PDF.js hands ș/ț decomposed (`U+0073 U+0326`) and î
recomposed (`U+00EE`), and the PDF path has no string comparison at
all. Zotero's word CFI on the NFD paragraph was right
(`epubcfi(/6/2!/4/8/1,:96,:107)`, 11 original characters for the
9-character NFC word).

**The bug (issue #74).** highlight-style.ts verifies the word position
by `displayedText(view, selector) === expected` (597) and the sentence
by `normText(segment.text)` against the displayed text (553/568);
`normText` (473) and `displayedText` (475) collapse whitespace only, no
Unicode normalization. On an NFD paragraph both fail: `hiddenPrimary`
is set (609), `_getSpotlightColor` answers `#00000000` for the segment
key (768), and `preparePiecesDOM` deletes both piece slots — nothing at
all is drawn. Then `repairWordPosition` returns at 591 when the
granularity is not word without resetting the flag; only the
`!state.popupOpen` branch (588) clears it, so the transparent primary
survives a switch to Sentence until the player is reopened. `logChange`
(266–271) dedupes per key for the instance's whole life, so the
`painting the primary transparent` line can be missing from a later
debug capture; `#00000000` is the proof either way. Severity minor: few
EPUBs are stored NFD.

**Beside the point.** Switching a playing PDF to Azure-Ioana MAI-Voice-2
got `Azure socket closed before turn.end` five times in a row
(00:33–00:34), manager and controller at `_error: "network"`, stuck on

## The highlight patch normalizes both sides to NFC before comparing, and the transparent flag clears when Word is left (2026-09-08 01:14, issue #74)

**The fix.** `normText` in highlight-style.ts — the one normalizer every
text comparison of the patch goes through (`resolvableSelector`,
`repairWordPosition`, the rewrite candidates of `repairSelectorForText`)
— NFC-normalizes before collapsing whitespace. Zotero's segment text is
already NFC; the displayed range's text now is too, so a document stored
decomposed passes the same checks as an NFC one, Zotero's correct word
position stays, and the head and tail pieces are built from the ranges
as before. And `repairWordPosition` resets `hiddenPrimary` whenever the
DOM granularity is not word, instead of only on a closed popup — at
sentence the primary is the sentence itself. Two tests in
highlight-style.test.ts: a `fakeRangeDOM` whose text is NFD with an NFC
segment text (the word "începe", 6 code points against 7) must draw the
word color with head and tail; a transparent primary must come back the
moment `_effectivePrimaryGranularity` returns sentence. Both read
`#00000000` before the change.

**Weighed and set aside.** Normalizing at each comparison site — three
edits for one effect, and a fourth site would miss it. Dropping the
transparent path — it is issue #4's, where Zotero's position really is
the wrong text. Translating offsets between the forms — the plugin only
compares the displayed text, never slices it by segment offsets, and the
pieces are ranges.

**Verified** on 1.11.3-beta2 through the bridge (Zotero 10.0.2-beta.7,
macOS), the build proven by the `normalize("NFC")` call and the reset
branch in the installed bundle. On the NFD paragraph at Word,
`_getSpotlightColor('ReadAloudActiveSegment')` = `#3478f6b3` and head +
word + tail reconstruct the segment after NFC (was `#00000000`, no
pieces); no `does not resolve to the sentence` or `painting the primary
transparent` line for the EPUB on a fresh instance. The transparent flag
was reproduced for real — one same-length ASCII substitution in the
paragraph's text node makes the word position resolve to text the
segment lacks, `#00000000` — and a switch to Sentence gave `#ffff00b3`
on the next segment; the pref write alone does not repaint, since
`repairWordPosition` runs on a state push. Azure-Alina timed every word
in all three encodings in the EPUB and the PDF (N = word count on all
26 segments); the English fixture at Word unchanged. Two things the
checklist draft had wrong, corrected in the file: on an EPUB at Word
`sentenceSlot` reads `"empty"` by design — the pieces carry the sentence
and Zotero's own slot is cleared (highlight-style.ts 739–746) — and
`"ours"` is the PDF path's value; paragraph 2's first sentence has 7
words, not 8.

**Seen beside it, not this fix's.** A PDF view whose player was toggled
about half a second after the tab opened stayed `patched: false` for
its whole session — Zotero's own pale word highlight, no sentence
highlight — until the player was closed and reopened, after which
`patched: true`, `sentenceSlot: "ours"`. `attach()` picks the patch by
`Array.isArray(view._pages)` and adds to `patchedViews` only on success
(highlight-style.ts 847–854), so a view that is not yet a laid-out PDF
at attach time stays unpatched until something calls `attach` again.
The ro PDF opened the same way minutes earlier was patched. Its own
issue.

**Checklist.** Section 3 gained items 16–18: the NFD paragraph at Word,
the flag clearing on a switch to Sentence, Azure timestamps across the
three encodings; Teardown is 19.

## The reading guard's dialog closes the players itself: the headphone button's close from the pane, an html:dialog with two buttons, and "open" widened to the popup (2026-09-08 12:49, issue #71)

The guard of issue #11 refused five settings changes while any tab had
a Read Aloud session, and its dialog only named the tabs. Since 1.11.4
it asks — *Stop reading and continue* / *Cancel* — and Stop closes
every open player and lets the caller's write follow. What the build
and its live run (1.11.4-beta2, Zotero 10.0.2-beta.7, Windows)
established:

- **Closing from the settings pane works, and it is quick.**
  `reader._internalReader.toggleReadAloudPopup(false)` on each of
  `Zotero.Reader._readers`, called from the pane's own code
  (read-aloud/player-stop.ts, wired in ui/prefs-pane.ts), flips
  `popupOpen` and `_readAloudManager.active` in the same tick, and the
  whole Stop click — dialog closed, players closed, pref written — lands
  within ~170–200 ms; the `.read-aloud-popup` element leaves the DOM on
  React's next render, within a second. Measured on the user's two real
  players (paused, a Premium voice) and on fixtures. Every plugin hook
  that rides on a close sees the sequence a click on the headphone
  button produces.
- **"Open" is `popupOpen || active`.** After `toggleReadAloudPopup(true)`
  the popup is up in ~10 ms and the manager activates ~1 s later on a
  first open (the voice resolve); the first-run popup never activates.
  Either way the popup has built its voice list, so the guard and the
  stop both count it. `_readAloudManager.paused` reads `true` on a
  manager that never ran — `diagnostics.players()` reports it as `null`
  while `open` is false.
- **The pane's html:dialog with two buttons.** `showModal()` focuses the
  first button; a `focus()` right after moves it to Cancel, so Enter
  cancels (a trusted Enter: `keydown()` 2, consumed). A trusted Escape is
  consumed by no JS handler at all (`keydown()` 0): Gecko's own dialog
  cancel fires `close`, which resolves the question false. The OS-prompt
  fallback is `Services.prompt.confirmEx` with the Stop label on button
  0 and Cancel as button 1 and the default.
- **The guard is async now**, and every caller awaits it; with nothing
  reading it still answers on a microtask, so a handler that read
  synchronous expectations (the tests' `fire` then `expect`) needs a
  `setTimeout(0)` flush first.
- **The provider's second ask keeps the check.** Local's connection
  check takes ~700 ms; a session opened in the same script as the
  Enable click (no await between) is open when the check lands, the
  post-check dialog appears, and Stop writes the pref with the
  "Connected. 68 voices available…" line kept — the outcome is dropped
  only on Cancel.
- **A reopened player lands one segment early, and on a never-played
  session it compounds.** The dialog and the README first said "picks
  up at the same sentence"; the live run measured the reopen of a PDF
  fixture one segment before where the manager stood, and a
  close/reopen loop on a script-started, skip-moved session went
  4 → 3 → 2 → 1 → 0, the plugin's stored rect following each landing.
  Reproduced with `toggleReadAloudPopup(false)` / `(true)` alone — no
  guard, no dialog, no pref write — so it is Zotero's own
  `savedPosition` restore (the 2026-08-27 off-by-one, seen then on the
  plugin's resume and compensated there by `resumeTarget`), now seen on
  the headphone path, where the plugin does nothing. Untested after real
  playback, which advances the position far past one segment per cycle.
  The wording is "picks up there" / 「从原处继续」 because of it; the
  checklist's item 5.3 (resume) is where it belongs.
- **Verifying this closes the user's own players.** Stop is global by
  design, and a player reopened by script is mute for its life (the
  autoplay gate, 2026-09-06), so nothing but the headphone button
  restores them: a run needs the user's consent, given here. Two more
  things for the next brief: a fixture session on an `en` document
  rewrites `reader.readAloudVoices` (`en.voice`, `en.tierVoices.local`,
  846 → 837 chars), to be snapshotted and rebuilt; and Zotero may unload
  a background reader tab mid-run (`reader-unloaded`), dropping it from
  `_readers` until the tab is selected again.
- **Seen, no issue.** The Cloudflare *Account ID* field in the pane is a
  plain text input — a full-window screenshot carries it — while the
  token beside it is masked; an account id is not a credential, but it
  is the one identifier in the pane that is neither a name nor a URL
  and is not masked.

**Checklist.** Section 3 item 9 was rewritten: the two buttons, the
Cancel and Stop paths with their latencies, `players()` /
`players(true)`, the provider's two asks, the reopen caveat, and the
consent rule.

## The stop key takes no reader, its toast has two homes, and the recorder's Escape rules out Shift+Esc as a default (2026-09-08 13:28, issue #71)

The second half of #71: `Shift+S` (`shortcuts.stopReading`) closes the
Read Aloud player in every tab of every window — the reading guard's
own `stopAll()` (read-aloud/player-stop.ts) — and says how many in a
toast. Built as 1.11.4-beta3/beta4 and run live through the bridge on
Zotero 10.0.2-beta.7 (Windows), two fixture sessions on a free voice.

- **A key that needs no reader.** Every other shortcut goes through
  `pickReader` — the speaking reader, else the selected tab's — and so
  does nothing on the library tab unless something is speaking (a
  paused player in a background tab picks nothing). The stop key skips
  the "no reader, no key" rule in `handleKeyDown`
  (ui/read-aloud-shortcuts.ts) and acts on `Zotero.Reader._readers`
  whole: from `zotero-pane` with two paused players a trusted Shift+S
  was consumed (`keydown()` 1) and closed both. It is the first key that
  works with the library tab selected.
- **The toast's two homes.** With a reader picked, `toastDoc(reader)` as
  the speed toast: the reader's iframe document, or the chrome window's
  when that tab is hidden. With none picked, the document the key was
  pressed in — the listener's `currentTarget.document`, the chrome
  window's from the library tab. Measured: from the reader the toast
  sat in tab A's iframe document and the chrome document had no
  `#ztts-speed-toast` element at all; from the library tab it sat in
  the chrome document while the readers' own, faded, stayed as they
  were. The element is created on first use per document and reused,
  the old text left in it at opacity 0; a read within ~800 ms of a press
  sees the new text at opacity 1.
- **The close is synchronous**: both readers `open: false` at the first
  poll sample after the press, as with the dialog's button.
- **Consumed only with a player open.** With none, or with the binding
  cleared, `keydown()` returned 0 and nothing else moved
  (`_state.primaryViewState` identical, no find popup, no dialog). The
  TIP's return value is the decisive measure here — the reader has no
  meaning of its own for Shift+S (its Shift bindings are
  `Cmd+Shift+R/L/G/Z` and `Shift+arrows`, `reader.js:81736-82110`; the
  chrome keysets carry `Shift+F3` only), so the key is free and stays
  free when unbound.
- **Why not Shift+Esc.** The recorder takes Escape as cancel
  (ui/shortcut-recorder.ts): a default on Shift+Esc could never be
  recorded back once cleared. Shift+S it is.
- **The recorder** refuses `Shift+S` on another row with `Already used by
  "Stop reading everywhere".` and leaves that row's binding alone; a
  trusted Escape cancels a recording and is consumed (`keydown()` 1) —
  the recorder's own capturing listener.
- **`diagnostics.stopKey()`**: `taken` first read "something to stop",
  true even with the binding cleared; since beta4 it is "bound and
  something to stop", so an empty `shortcut` gives `taken: false`.
  `stopKey(true)` routes as a press on the main window (`pickReader` on
  `mainWindows()[0]`) and reports the count, what is left open and the
  toast's text.
- **Two things for the next brief.** `readAloud.memory` is an undeclared
  pref and is not among the settings a backup carries, so pointing it
  at a free voice for a run never reaches WebDAV; but every shortcut
  pref change is a settings change and fires the auto-upload (three in
  this run). And the memory-sync observer moves the memory back by
  itself once `reader.readAloudVoices` is rebuilt to its baseline.

**Checklist.** Section 4 gained item 9: the diagnostic, the press in the
reader and from the library tab, the toast's document, the singular,
the fall-through, the recorder row and its conflict message, the key
free with the binding cleared, and what the item touches.

## Zotero rebuilds the controller when its voice list lands, even onto the voice already playing, and the plugin's catalog makes that land mid-sentence (2026-09-08 15:31, issue #75)

Reported as "in a PDF, Shift+Space starts reading and after the third
word it starts again from the first word". Reproduced on 1.11.4-beta4
(Zotero 10.0.2-beta.7, Windows, fixture-a on Kokoro) with a sampler on
the manager every 100 ms for 12 s after a trusted press; fixed in
1.11.4-beta5 by read-aloud/unchanged-voice.ts.

- **The mechanism is Zotero's.** `_prepareReadAloud` runs `loadVoices()`
  on every popup open (reader.js:84260); `loadVoices` ends in
  `_resolveVoice()` (82310), whose "if current voice is still valid, keep
  it" branch calls `_applyVoice()` (82428-82432); `_applyVoice`, with the
  manager active and its segments in place, calls `_createController()`
  unconditionally (82497-82524), which destroys the speaking controller
  and starts the segment over from `backwardStopIndex` (82644-82656). On
  a tab's first open `_allVoices` is empty, nothing plays until the list
  lands, and the resolve runs before playback — no restart. On every
  later open the list is still populated, playback starts at once, and
  the fresh list lands mid-sentence: the playback clock
  (`_controller._audioContext.currentTime`) went 1.08 → 0, the
  controller object was new, the word highlight replayed the sentence's
  first rects, and the log carried a second `volume gain inserted`
  right after `hid 9 system voices from the Local tier` plus the same
  `local: 12 word timestamps for 50 chars (cached)` line twice.
  Reproduced with Zotero's own `Ctrl+Shift+R` as readily as with
  `Shift+Space`, and unchanged with `sameForAllDocuments` and
  `globalSpeed` off — nothing of the plugin's is on the path (memory-
  sync's `_resolveVoice` own-property hook passes through).
- **The plugin sets the width of the window.** `loadVoices` awaits the
  remote provider — with the plugin, the composite interface: Zotero's
  own voices GET (200 at +14, +105, +203 ms) plus every enabled
  provider's listing, five here, 2333 voices — and the merged list was
  ready 601, 689 and 1190 ms after Zotero's 200. Without the plugin the
  rebuild lands within ~200 ms of the open, inside the first word;
  with it, 1.2–1.6 s in, three to six words at 1.7×. The window is as
  long as the slowest listing: this profile's first open of the fixture
  logged `Zotero's Read Aloud voices did not arrive within 20 s` and
  three providers' `no voice list within 15 s` — a listing that answers
  at ten seconds restarts the sentence at ten seconds.
- **The same branch restarted the sentence on a tier chip of the
  current tier and on a dropdown pick of the voice already playing** —
  the #11 measurement's "one tier chip, new controller" was this
  branch too.
- **The fix** shadows `_applyVoice` on each tab's manager prototype
  (proto-patches, the pauses/volume shape): active, a controller,
  segments, `_voice.id === _voiceID`, the id still in `_allVoices` and
  `voicesForLanguage`, `segmentGranularity` unchanged → set `_voice`
  from the new list and `_selectedTier`, return without
  `_createController`; anything else runs Zotero's method. A granularity
  change does not destroy the controller at once in Zotero's own path
  either — it nulls the segments and asks the view for new ones
  (`onRequestSegments`), and the controller goes when they land.
  `diagnostics.unchangedVoice()` reports `kept` per tab; the live proof
  is `kept` 0 → 1 on a popup reopen with one `volume gain inserted` per
  start and the clock monotonic. Verified on 1.11.4-beta6: one
  controller over 13 s on a reopen by `Shift+Space` and by
  `Ctrl+Shift+R`, the clock 0.56 → 1.82 across the band where beta5
  reset at 1.35 s, `kept` 1 then 2; a real `selectVoice` still rebuilds
  in 30–45 ms with `kept` unchanged; `selectTier('local')` keeps
  (`kept` 3).
- **Every plugin upgrade used to restart a reading tab's sentence.** The
  in-place install's re-attach re-resolves the voice on a live session;
  on beta5 that destroyed and recreated the user's paused Premium
  controller, on beta6 the tab read `kept: 1` right after the install,
  untouched. Zotero's own voices too. A checklist therefore reads `kept`
  as an increment: a tab nobody touched can already carry one.
- **`find` / `some` on a reader-realm array with a sandbox callback answer
  `undefined` / `false` without a throw.** The first build of the shadow
  (1.11.4-beta5) was inert live for exactly that: `_allVoices.find(v =>
  v.id === id)` and `voicesForLanguage.some(…)` — the lists are the
  reader realm's (`Array.isArray` true, `constructor === Array` false),
  their methods cannot call a bare sandbox function, and the guard
  returned null on every call while Zotero's own `_applyVoice` runs the
  identical idiom happily inside its realm. No error in the console, no
  line in the log; only the sampler's "controller new" said anything.
  Index loops over the same arrays find the voice (system-voices.ts had
  walked `_allVoices` that way since #17). The unit test had passed on
  a same-realm fake; it now has a foreign-array case whose `find` /
  `some` answer nothing. The rulebook's "filter → []" is the same
  failure; a callback handed to a reader-realm array method is never
  called.
- **Worth reporting upstream**: a "keep it" that keeps it is the fix in
  Zotero; the shadow becomes a no-op the day it lands. The forum text is
  in the issue's closing comment.

**Checklist.** Section 3 gained the reopen item: one controller per
start, `unchangedVoice().kept`, the voice-change and tier-chip
contrasts.

## Zotero's two views read the Read Aloud position lock differently, and a re-emitted state can never satisfy the DOM view's gate (2026-09-08 17:07, issue #76)

**The user reported that Shift+Enter does not scroll an EPUB back to the
sentence being read** — the view returns only when the next sentence
starts. It is worse than that when paused: it never returns at all.

**The lock is one call, the response two.** `returnToSpoken`
(`read-aloud-shortcuts.ts:321`) locks the view
(`_lockPositionToReadAloud()`, `reader.js:84174`, forwarding to
`this._lastView?.lockPositionToReadAloud()`) and then re-emits the
manager's state (`manager._stateChanged()`) so the locked view has a
push to act on. Both arrive: the lock goes up 2 ms after the key press,
the push reaches the view at 61 ms. What differs is what each view does
with them.

- **PDF view** — `setReadAloudState()` (`reader.js:76411`) navigates
  whenever `!state.annotationPopup && this._readAloudPositionLocked`
  (`76459`). No segment change needed; a bare re-emit is enough, and the
  navigate branch was measured running with `segmentChanged` false.
- **DOM view (EPUB, snapshot)** — `class ReadAloud` (`reader.js:53191`)
  computes `segmentChanged = state.activeSegment !==
  previousState?.activeSegment` (`53236`) and gates on
  `segmentChanged && !state.annotationPopup && this.positionLocked`
  (`53240`). `setPositionLocked` (`53276`) only raises the flag, and
  only while `this.state?.active`.

**The gate can never open on a re-emit.**
`_composeReadAloudStateSnapshot()` (`reader.js:83913`) puts
`activeSegment: manager.activeSegment` into the snapshot — the same
object reference — so along the whole path (`_stateChanged` `82731` →
`_onReadAloudEngineStateChanged` `83860` → `_updateState` `83319` →
`_pushReadAloudToViews` `83380`/`83907` → `view.setReadAloudState`
`83910`) `segmentChanged` is false by construction. Of the three
conditions at `53240`, only that one was ever false — measured, not
inferred: `positionLocked` true, `annotationPopup` null, `popupOpen`
true, `hasActiveSegment` true in every run.

**Measured.** Playing: 5468 ms of nothing, then the return at the
segment change. Paused, 3.9 screens away: 110 samples over 6.5 s and 70
over 7.5 s with `scrollY` at one distinct value, the scroll flag never
up; a paginated EPUB 7 pages away, the same over 3.46 s. With the lock
set by that paused key press, one forced `skipAhead` returned the view
in 167 ms, still paused — which is also why the player's own skip
buttons come back and our key does not.

**A programmatic scroll unlocks exactly like a user's.**
`_onManualNavigation → setPositionLocked(false)` (`reader.js:55162`)
fires on `iframeWindow.scrollTo(...)` and `navigateToNextPage()` alike,
so a test that scrolls away to set up is genuinely starting unlocked —
no need to force the flag, though forcing it false first proves a later
true came from the key.

## Driving a real Shift+Enter into a reader from the bridge, and why pixels are the wrong measure here (2026-09-08 17:07, issue #76)

**A trusted key needs two things the obvious form misses.** Through
`nsITextInputProcessor` bound to `reader._iframeWindow`:

- **Focus must be inside the reader.** With focus on the library item
  tree the event arrives at the main window with `target: "div"`,
  `defaultPrevented` false and `keydown` returning 0 — the plugin
  declines it. `Zotero_Tabs.select(tabID)` + `reader.focus()` first.

## A write of Zotero's highlight-level pref repaints every open reader inside the write, and a bare h during a session annotates (2026-09-08 22:30, issue #67)

Research for issue #67 — a key that flips the Read Aloud highlight
between word and sentence — before any plan. A tester run on the released
1.11.5, Zotero 10.0.2-beta.7 on macOS, `Azure-en-US-ChristopherNeural`
(real word timestamps), `test/fixtures/fixture-a.pdf` and
`test/fixtures/return-key/return-key.epub`.

**The pref is the whole mechanism.** Zotero keeps the level in
`extensions.zotero.reader.readAloud.highlightGranularity` (`paragraph |
sentence | word`, default `sentence`, `defaults/preferences/zotero.js:1814`),
and its settings pane is a `preference=`-bound menulist on it
(`preferences_general.xhtml:227-236`, "Highlight current" / 高亮当前, the
values labeled `word` / `sentence` / `paragraph`, 单词 / 句子 / 段落). Each
`ReaderInstance` reads it at construction (`xpcom/reader.js:268` → the
bundle's initial state at `reader.js:82980`, guarded by an `includes` over
the three values) and registers its own observer (`xpcom/reader.js:671` →
`_handleReadAloudHighlightGranularityChange`, `:1241-1245`), which calls
the bundle's `setReadAloudHighlightGranularity` (`reader.js:83938`): an
early return on an unchanged or invalid value, else `_updateState` with a

every view (`:83378` → `_pushReadAloudToViews`, `:83907`); the DOM helper's

(`:76411`) through `_resolveReadAloudPrimaryPosition` (`:76492`, switch at
`:76532`) and `_render()` (`:76455`). Both take the word from

**Measured: the repaint lands inside the write.** Pref observers fire
