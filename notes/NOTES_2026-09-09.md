## Speechify joined as a provider of its own: the locale in the voice id, one request at a time, and marks aligned by their text (2026-09-09 01:55, issue #79)

**The API, measured with the owner's key on the Free plan.** Base
`https://api.speechify.ai` (`api.sws.speechify.com` answers the same),
`Authorization: Bearer sk_…`. A wrong or missing key is 401
`{ error: { code: "unauthorized", message: "Unauthorized" }, request_id }`
on every route, the voice list included, so the list is the connection
check. Refusals are `{ error: { code, message }, request_id }`: 400
`validation_failed` (an empty input; an unknown model, the message listing
the valid ones; a bad `audio_format` / `output_format`, the message listing
the accepted values; **"Field input must not exceed 2000 characters"**),
400 `bad_request` "no speakable text" for whitespace, 404
`voice_not_found`, 429 `rate_limited` / `concurrency_limit_reached` with
`Retry-After: 1` and a `docs_url`, 502 `upstream_failure` "Bad Gateway".
Every reply carries `speechify-request-id` and `ratelimit-limit` /
`-remaining` / `-reset` (10 on the speech route, 20 on the list routes).

- **Voices.** `GET /v1/voices` is paginated — `{ voices, next_cursor,
  has_more }`, 50 a page by default, 200 at most — and lists 992 shared
  voices in five pages (115–125 KB and 300–450 ms each). Every voice has
  one `locale`, the same its `models[].languages` list; no voice is
  multi-language. 36 locales, no Mandarin (ten `yue-CN`), 496 male and
  496 female, five display names shared by two voices each (`dominic`,
  `dominic_32`). The 127 English voices, exactly, list `simba-3.2` beside
  `simba-3.0`; the other 865 list `simba-3.0` alone. `?model=`, `?locale=`
  (a prefix) and `?type=shared|personal` filter server-side; this account
  has no `personal` (cloned) voice.
- **Models.** `GET /v1/audio/models`: `simba-3.0`, the default ("routed
  by the request `language`") and `simba-3.2`, recommended, English only.
  `simba-english` and `simba-multilingual` — what the SDK examples still
  name — answer 400 `model_retired` on this workspace's API version
  (2026-09-29): retired 2026-09-21, off for every version on 2026-11-21.
  The models guide disclaims Chinese, Japanese and Korean, yet the ja-JP,
  ko-KR and yue-CN voices answer audio with per-word marks.
- **Speech.** `POST /v1/audio/speech` `{ input, voice_id, model?,
  language?, audio_format?, output_format?, options? }` answers JSON
  `{ audio_data (base64), audio_format, billable_characters_count,
  speech_marks }`. The default `wav` is 48 kHz mono 16-bit under a
  streamed header whose `data` chunk declares 26 bytes (`wav_24000` the
  same, RIFF size `0xFFFFFFFF`) — not a header a parser can trust; `mp3`
  is 128 kbps (141 KB for a 157-character sentence, 191 KB once base64);
  `output_format: mp3_24000_64` halves it; `pcm_24000` is raw 16-bit LE
  mono. Latency 0.7 s for "Hi", 1.1–2.2 s for 44–157 characters on either
  model, 13.2 s for 1,900 (95 s of speech). `billable_characters_count`
  is the input length, spaces counted, SSML tags not (a 74-character
  `<speak>` wrapper billed 28); plain text with `<`, `&` and quotes is
  spoken literally.
- **Speech marks.** One `type: "sentence"` object with `chunks` of
  `type: "word"`, each `{ start, end, start_time, end_time, value }`; the
  times are **milliseconds** (11260 for an 11.26 s clip — the reference
  page that says seconds is wrong), the offsets index the input. For Latin
  text every chunk of every test matched `input.slice(start, end)` — a
  chunk being sometimes several words (`Tom & Jerry`, `Dr. Smith's`) and
  `simba-3.0` sometimes skipping some (`'s résumé`, `)`) where `simba-3.2`
  timed every word. **For CJK text the offsets drift by one per
  full-width punctuation mark**: after `。` every `start` is one too high,
  after the next `，` two, after the next `。` three, while the `value`s
  are right and the sentence's own `value` comes back re-spaced with a `.`
  appended. Hence the words go through `alignWordsToText` by their text,
  the offsets ignored. `simba-3.2` given Chinese answers audio and marks
  that cover nothing (no chunks in one run, five ending at 1.9 s in
  another).
- **`language`.** Optional; anything is accepted, `xx-YY` too. It changes
  the output: the same Chinese sentence on `george` with `simba-3.0` came
  back 3.3 s long with `language: zh-CN` and 8.1 s without; a Japanese
  voice given Japanese 5.3–5.4 s either way, Korean 4.4–4.5 s. The plugin
  sends the voice's own locale.
- **Limits** (`docs.speechify.ai/docs/get-started/api-limits`, and
  measured). Free: 1 simultaneous request, 1 per second sustained, a
  burst of 10; Starter ($10 a month, 1,000,000 characters): 15
  simultaneous, 20 per second. Three syntheses sent at once: one 200 and
  two 429 `concurrency_limit_reached` ("your plan allows 1 simultaneous
  requests on this API") in 330 ms; twelve two-character requests at
  once: one 200, nine of those, two `rate_limited`. `* * *` answered 502
  after 60.3 s once and after 0.3 s once; `***`, `—`, `1.` and `§ 2`
  answered 0.04–0.9 s of audio.
- **Price** (speechify.ai/pricing). Free: 50,000 characters a month, a
  hard cap, no card. Starter $10 a month for 1,000,000 and $10 per further
  million; Pro $99 for 3,000,000; Scale $499 for 10,000,000. **What the
  cap answers is documented nowhere and unmeasured**: the provider reads
  402, and a 403 or 429 whose code or message names the allowance, as
  `quota` — a guess, to be confirmed the day someone hits it.
- **Streaming.** `/v1/audio/stream` is raw `audio/mpeg` without marks
  (3.8 s for 86 characters, slower than the JSON route);
  `/v1/audio/stream/with-timestamps` exists. Neither fits Read Aloud's
  one finished blob per segment.

**The plugin** (`src/core/providers/speechify.ts`).

- The published id is `<locale>/<id>` (`en-US/george`, `ja-JP/akari`): a
  provider is built per call (`src/index.ts` `getProvider`) and has no
  memory of the listing, and the locale is all synthesis needs —
  `modelForLocale` (`en*` → `simba-3.2`, else `simba-3.0`, the API's own
  rule) and the `language` hint; a voice without a locale goes under `mul`
  and gets the multilingual model and no hint. The label is `display_name
  (gender)`, the id appended where two voices share both.
- `SerialQueue`, one per module (`sharedQueue`), first come first served:
  every request — the list pages too, since "this API" in the 429 covers
  them — waits for the one before it; a task whose signal aborted while it
  waited (the remote interface's timeout) is rejected unsent. A 429 waits
  `Retry-After` (1 s without one, 5 s at most), up to three times, unless
  its body names the allowance; a 5xx is asked once more after 500 ms, as
  Cloudflare's is. The result's `note` carries the model and what happened
  (`simba-3.2 after 2 rate-limit waits`), and `remote-interface.ts` now
  prints the note on the line with the word count too — `speechify: 12
  word timestamps for 58 chars (simba-3.2)` — the only place a live run
  sees the routing or a retry.
- Text with no letter and no digit (`/[\p{L}\p{N}]/u`) answers empty
  audio without a request, and the remote interface's `size === 0` branch
  plays the 400 ms pause (issue #42's path, where Azure answered no audio
  for the same text).
- Over 2,000 characters, `splitForSpeechify` cuts at the last sentence end
  (`[。！？]`, or `[.!?]` before whitespace) before the cap, else the last
  whitespace, else the cap; the pieces are asked as `pcm_24000` and joined
  by `wav.ts` `pcm16ToWav` — Speechify's own WAV header cannot be trusted
  — with the later pieces' words shifted by the earlier pieces' durations
  (bytes / 48,000). Unit-tested, not exercised live: Zotero's sentence
  segmentation rarely makes such a segment.
- `checkSynthesis` is "Hi" on the voice. Test connection lists (five
  pages; its `voices available` count is the key's whole list), then
  probes with the first voice listed (`aadi`, hi-IN), through the queue.
- The class of 400 that quotes a limit — "must not exceed 2000
  characters" — is the request's, never the plan's: `isQuota` reads only
  402, 403 and 429, and never the two 429 codes the pace limits answer
  with, whose messages speak of the plan too.

**Verified live on 1.11.7-beta** (2026-09-09, macOS, Zotero
10.0.2-beta.7, the owner's key on the Free plan, 512 characters spent):
the pane, `Connected. 992 voices available. Synthesis works.` in 3.0 s,
`sk_wrong` refused in 0.33 s with the 401 quoted; the browser's Local
tier 700 → 1692 with 84 / 37 / 51 / 44 / 36 / 10 under en-US / en-GB /
fr-FR / ja-JP / ko-KR / yue-CN and none under Multiple languages, one
sample 29,229 bytes of MP3 for the 52-character sample text; a fixture
session on `speechify::en-US/george` active in 2.1 s with the first
audio 1.7 s later, every segment line ending `(simba-3.2)`,
`activeWordTimestamp: "real"` under the highlight, and not one 429 or
retry in the log; `* * *` a 6,444-byte pause in 4 ms with no request;
a wrong key mid-reading answered in 0.29 s. Two things the run taught:
a session the bridge starts by a synthetic key sits at position 0 with
its AudioContext `suspended` (Gecko's autoplay gate, checklist section
0), so the audio and the pace of the highlight are the owner's check;
and a profile with *Upload settings automatically* on uploads every
pref write to its WebDAV settings file — a verification run puts the
key there for its minutes, and the restore's upload clears it.

**Open.** The free cap's answer, above. Whether the Cantonese, Japanese
and Korean voices produce speech worth listing is the owner's ears' call
(the clips were handed over; the docs disclaim the languages). English
voices under *Multiple languages*, if the ears say the multilingual
model's Chinese is usable.
